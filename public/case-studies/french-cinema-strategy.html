Case Study

French Cinema Streaming Strategy

Python · Pandas · Matplotlib · Seaborn

Brief

A hypothetical content-strategy brief: Canopy, a boutique streaming service curating French-language film, wants to know whether its niche actually holds up. Working from a 15,069-title streaming metadata dataset, the question was whether French films genuinely differ from the rest of the catalogue on runtime, ratings and genre range — or whether "French cinema" is really just a marketing story.

Cleaning

Rotten Tomatoes scores arrived as percentage strings ("68%") and were converted to decimals for comparison against IMDb. French films were isolated as any title whose Country mentioned France or whose Language mentioned French — 1,070 titles against 13,999 everything-else, with the usual scattered gaps in Age, Rotten Tomatoes and Director fields left as missing rather than guessed at.

1. Are French films actually longer?

Yes — French films run 101.5 minutes on average against 92.8 minutes for everything else, a 8.7-minute gap. Enough to support a "substantive, feature-length" angle rather than a padded runtime.

2. Do they actually rate better?

French films outscore the rest on both platforms: 6.37 vs 5.87 on IMDb (+0.50) and 68.2% vs 65.0% on Rotten Tomatoes (+3.2 points). The distribution view below is raw counts, not normalised — non-French has roughly 13× more titles, so its bars sit taller throughout — but the French bars still sit visibly further right on both charts, so the gap isn't a handful of outliers dragging an average.

3. How much genre range is there?

76.2% of French films (815 of 1,070) carry more than one genre tag, and two or three genres is the most common pattern — a single film can plausibly serve more than one audience interest rather than locking into one category.

Reading those numbers as a content strategy, the case for Canopy holds up:

  • 01 Higher ratings on both platforms support quality-led marketing rather than a "hidden gem" apology.
  • 02 Longer average runtime positions the catalogue as substantive, feature-length viewing rather than filler.
  • 03 High genre overlap means individual titles can be marketed across more than one audience segment.
  • 04 A small (1,070-title) catalogue is a real constraint — the strategy is quality and positioning, not volume.
